# Review of PLAN.md (v0.3, 2026-10-06, including E.4a)

Reviewer basis: PLAN.md; `druckenmiller_fatpitch_reference_1.md` (§2, §3, §7); library notes present at review time (Citi/Feig 2009, CNBC 2013-09, Delivering Alpha 2014, CNBC 2015-03, CNBC 2016-11, CNBC 2020-11; Lost Tree, Real Vision and NBIM entries are catalog-only stubs that defer to a `reference_sources` directory that is still empty); spot-checks of amber and CBP code. Tags used below: **[V]** verified in a cited file or library note; **[I]** inference or reviewer knowledge not yet backed by a library note.

## Verdict

Thesis-first is the correct inversion of the Automated Alpha cascade and the three-lift split with a separate `fatpitch` package is sound. The seven-step model nevertheless mis-places price action: the primary sources (Feig 2009 [V]) describe the chart as a hard entry veto, a continuous exit input and a leading indicator ("momentum tools bottom 12–18 months before fundamentals"), not a stage-4 confirmation applied after the thesis. Stage 4 also imports gates (forensics, ROIC, Form 4/13F "smart money", contrarian COT) that trace to the article, not to Druckenmiller, while the one sizing rule he states repeatedly (size to market liquidity, scale with hot/cold) produces no output at all. The fidelity design is weaker than it looks: ~30–60 cases cluster into roughly ten macro episodes, the ±1-quarter window and 11-sector rank correlations make trivial baselines score well, and no null model is specified, so the E.4 targets cannot currently show that the engine beats "always long when the Fed is easing." The 12–24-month forward view as specified (ZQ/SR3 futures path) cannot be replayed before 2018–2021 with free data, so E3's 1970→ replay would run a different engine than production. E.4a improves the pre-2002 problem but anchors on WALCL−TGA−RRP, a construction Druckenmiller did not use historically; his own long-history liquidity rule (money growth vs industrial production, Feig 2009 [V]) is a stated rule with data to 1959 and should replace the proxy search as the era-B measure. Several plan claims about amber and CBP are slightly off (listed in the table); none is blocking. The D.5 decisions need a second look: U3 as decided (IBKR bars re-pulled, never stored) makes live futures decisions non-replayable and splits the price path between validation and production, and the U4 proxy set measures valuation and credit stress rather than the issuance-quality gauges he named, while free series with the needed history (Ritter, SIFMA, Z.1) are omitted. Recommended: fix E.1 steps 4–6, define scoring rules and null models before E2, decouple the policy-path projection from futures, set a numeric E3 go/no-go, and take options U3-B and U4-C below.

## Section-by-section assessment

### A. Fidelity of the process model (E.1)

Verified against sources:

- Liquidity vs earnings: the plan's step 1 matches Lost Tree/Feig/2014 [V]. Two corrections: the longest-history liquidity rule he states is money growth vs industrial production (Feig 2009), not Fed assets − TGA − RRP; and the 2020 "net" framing is Fed purchases vs Treasury issuance. Earnings matter at the security level (2000: Hyman model earnings −36% vs Street +18% was part of the 2-year-note thesis, Feig 2009 [V]), so "earnings do not move the market" is an index-level statement only.
- Forward view: 18-month visualisation is stated; the method is charts (daily/weekly/monthly second-derivative momentum; "monthly charts predict 8–20 months") and fundamentals, not a futures strip. The engine's projection should be built from the same inputs (curve, trend in liquidity inputs, policy direction), which also makes it replayable (finding 4).
- Price action: leading input and veto, not confirmation (finding 1). "Technicals degraded later" (reference §7.1) argues for lower weight on *entry timing*, not for demoting the veto.
- Invest then investigate: finding 7.
- Concentration/sizing: finding 3.
- Reversal: finding 8. Also hot/cold is stated (Feig 2009 [V]) and the plan's self-scorecard is correct; it should also feed the size band.
- Currency/bond focus: documented large bets are FX and bonds (1981, 1992, 2000 2y notes, 2013 yen, 2016 global bonds, 2022 bonds) with equities "rarely > 100% net long or 50% net short" and often expressed through regions or sectors. The plan's class ordering for E5 (commodities first) is a data-convenience choice; fidelity ordering should be rates/FX first because that is where his thesis tests live. Equities first in the 13F test is fine as a separate track.
- Valuation: context only, judged relative to policy ("extremely high… appropriately priced" given policy, 2015 [V]; "bubble appropriate given monetary policy", 2014 [V]). Never a trigger; it belongs in the fragility overlay as a non-gating attribute. CAPE (D-16) as "excess overlay proxy" is not his gauge; his gauges are issuance quality and whether excess has reached the banking system (2014 [V]).
- Contrarianism: he rejects it; the crowd is right 80% of the time (Feig 2009 [V]). Any gate that fades positioning is anti-fidelity.
- Catalysts: the sources show catalysts as the step from starter to full size (Schlesinger comments, 1992) and as thesis invalidators (2016 election). The plan has catalyst proximity only in conviction.

Over-weighted: stage 4 (seven gates, mostly article-origin). Under-weighted: chart veto, sizing, premise-based exits, cross-region policy. Missing: a size output; a premise ledger per thesis; the "concentric circles" expression rule (the second-order expressions made more money than the pound; expression ranking should include correlated instruments across classes, which the beta ranking in step 3 can do if drivers include the thesis instrument itself).

### B. Ground truth and metrics (E.3–E.4)

Corpus: dated public calls are biased toward televised bearishness and conference warnings; positions change between statements; many statements describe positions already held, so the entry as-of is earlier and often known only to the month. 13F covers one expression channel. Documented trades are the strongest ground truth but number roughly 10–15. The corpus is adequate for a *diagnostic* fidelity report, not for a statistical pass/fail at the targets stated.

Concrete scoring rule (pre-register in E1):

```
case:
  id, asof (information date, ET close), era (A|B), truth_type (process|action)
  source_reliability (primary|near-primary|secondary)
  episode_id
  targets:
    regime_direction   in {easing, tightening, neutral}      (optional)
    theses[]:          {asset_class, direction, region?, premise[]}
    expression:        instrument or instrument family
    action:            enter | size_up | exit | flat
    size_band:         optional, from documented sizes
  window: ±N business days (default 20; ±1 quarter only for 13F-derived cases)
score per case:
  regime: 1 if engine direction == target at asof (strict window), 0.5 if within window, else 0
  thesis: 1 if any engine thesis matches class+direction(+region if given) within window
  expression: 1 if target instrument family in top-3 at the first matching thesis date
  action: 1 if engine tier/exit state implies the same action
  log-score variant: use engine probability of target direction where available
aggregate: mean per episode first, then across episodes; report by era, by reliability, by truth_type
```

Null models to beat (each scored with the same rule): always risk-on; Fed-direction-only (sign of last fed funds change); 12-month trend of SPY/IEF/DXY; persistence (last state). Targets become "engine − best null ≥ 15 points on era A; era B reported, not gated." The 13F test should use QoQ changes (finding 11). Cash fidelity has perhaps five usable periods; report per period, no target.

### C. Point-in-time and leakage

Findings 13–15 plus: FRED monthly averages pre-1986 embed intra-month information (plan acknowledges); CBP research series are current-vintage (plan acknowledges) and INDPRO/UNRATE first releases exist in CBP (`unemployment_pit.py`, `fred_INDPRO_first_release.parquet` [V]) and should be mandatory for the M2−IP rule; Shiller earnings arrive with a lag of months; World Bank monthly averages publish early the following month; 13F information date is `filed`; S&P membership pre-2021 is survivorship-biased without D-9; rolling betas (step 3) must use published driver values. A single code path for replay and live (as CBP's golden harness does) is the only reliable guard; the plan's "as-of leak test" at E5 should be a property test at E0 on `Source` (no `published_at > asof` ever returned) rather than a late phase check.

### D. Calibration (E.6) and overfitting

Finding 10. Additional: the parameter cap must be a number; interpreted thresholds fitted on ~10 episodes will overfit at anything beyond a handful of parameters; prefer parameter *ranges* with the fidelity report shown across the range (the plan's ±20% sensitivity applied to fidelity, not only performance). Stated parameters are rare in reality (inflation thresholds 4.5%/5%, rates+oil+USD rising, no stops, 1–2 pitches/year, size caps from Feig); most of the engine will be interpreted, so the "stated never tuned" rule protects little. Record every scoring run via `prereg.record_run` so `n_trials` is known for DSR.

### E. Architecture

Separate package: correct, for the reasons the plan gives, with two amendments (findings 22–23): pin the nightly to a tagged build, and define the `Source` contract with `published_at`, ET timestamps and an atomic snapshot. Amber should ship a `FixtureSource` and the fidelity suite must run with no amber import. The `Decision` schema needs a version field and a stable id per signal for the exit ledger. `fatpitch_store.py` in amber as the only touchpoint is right.
