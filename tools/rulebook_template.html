Fat Pitch Rulebook

Every rule the engine uses to reproduce Druckenmiller's public investment process, where in his interviews and speeches each rule comes from, and how each threshold was chosen. Thesis first: liquidity and policy set the regime, the chart can veto, size follows conviction, and the default is cash.

Bar colour inside each step shows the mix of rule tags: green = stated by him, amber = interpreted, red = from the Automated Alpha article (weight 0).

Decisions and what they were expected to do

Every change to a rule, threshold or test is a recorded decision. Each record keeps the question in plain words, the options that were on the table, the evidence that settled it, and a prediction of its effect written before any result was seen. The measured result is added afterwards, next to the prediction, so a wrong expectation stays visible.

  1. Problem foundA rule misreads history, a test cannot be passed, or the owner asks for something new.
  2. Evidence gathered blindResearch reads the source library and public records without seeing case answers or scores.
  3. Options, one recommendedTwo to four choices in plain words; the recommendation states its reason and what it rejects.
  4. Owner decides; prediction writtenThe choice and its expected effect are committed before any run that could inform it.
  5. Measured one change at a timeEach change is its own commit and run, so its effect is the difference from the step before.

How criteria are set

stated

He said it

The principle appears in a primary or near-primary transcript in his own words. This tag is about the principle only. When he gave a number (4.5% inflation, gross leverage 4:1, 1–2 pitches a year), it is used as-is and never tuned. Most stated rules still contain numbers we chose; each rule card shows "numbers: his / ours", and the review list collects them.

interpreted

Built from what he said

The principle is his but the formula, window or threshold is not (for example, "money growing a lot faster than industrial production" needs a number for "a lot"). Each choice is written down with its reasoning and the source it rests on.

interpreted-from-article

Not his

Gates that come only from the Automated Alpha article (accounting forensics, ROIC, 13F/Form 4 "smart money", contrarian COT). They are computed and logged at weight 0 and turn on only if a primary citation is found.

tunable · max 8

Fit to his decisions, not to returns

Only 8 interpreted parameters may move. Each is chosen by leave-one-episode-out fidelity: how often the engine reaches the decision he actually made, using only data published at the time. To pass, the engine's regime odds must beat climatology, a 12-month trend and an always-easing forecaster on probability skill, call tightening better than always-easing, and track the Fed's own rate cycles; results are shown across each parameter's range.

Deciding a criterion

Applied to every candidate rule before it enters the spec.

  • Does a primary or near-primary transcript show him stating the principle?
    • YesDid he give a number?
      • Yesstated, fixed at his number
      • Nostated principle, interpreted formula: threshold set by reasoning and recorded
    • Only secondaryinterpreted until a transcript confirms it
    • Only the articleinterpreted-from-article, weight 0
  • Do sources disagree?
    • Same objectprimary beats secondary; otherwise widen to the stated range (horizon 18–36 months)
    • Different objectskeep both (internals lead 6–12 months; momentum bottoms 12–18 months)
  • Is the interpreted threshold among the 8 tunable?
    • Yessearch range fixed in advance → leave-one-episode-out fidelity → held-out score run once, pre-registered
    • Nofixed design choice, documented; sensitivity reported, never fitted
  • Was the rule written after looking at how cases turned out?
    • Guardthesis table authored before reading case outcomes; spec, table and case list hashed and registered before any scoring

Decision trees

Nightly, for each as-of date, using only data published by then. Missing data returns unknown, which never counts as pass or fail. Numbers are current registry values; tunable ones are marked.

Step 1 · Regime and policy

Liquidity and the Fed set the backdrop, per region (US, euro area, Japan, UK).

  • Which data era is the as-of date in?
    • 2002 →All three liquidity measures reported: M2 vs IP, Fed assets − TGA − RRP, Fed purchases − net Treasury issuance
    • Pre-2002M2 growth − industrial-production growth (his stated rule, 1959 →)
  • M2 yoy − IP yoy above 2.0 pp tunable?
    • YesLiquidity impulse positive
    • Below 0Liquidity impulse negative
    • BetweenNeutral. A sign change counts only after 2 consecutive readings
  • Policy direction: which way was the Fed's last rate move? (fed funds target from 1982, discount rate 1948–1982)
    • Up / downtightening / easing, held until the first move the other way ("stay long until the Fed tightens")
    • No move on recordWhich Fed balance-sheet programme is in force (announced releases since 2008)?
      • Buyingeasing, taper phases included
      • Runofftightening (QT)
      • Noneneutral; a programme ended within 6 months raises the QE-end setback flag
  • Odds of easing / on hold / tightening: votes from R-14 and the liquidity family, each outcome starting at 0.1
  • Hybrid track only, warning flag: anticipated Fed turn from the bill market (6-month vs 3-month bills) checked by inflation momentum
  • Fed funds − Taylor rate (unemployment-gap version) beyond ±2.0 pp tunable?
    • Below −2Policy too loose: policy-error opportunity
    • Above +2Policy too tight: policy-error opportunity
  • Inflation checks
    • CPI > 4.5% and tighteningNo-soft-landing flag: recession prior
    • CPI > 5% and FF < CPIInflation veto flag: long duration and long equity lose one evidence family
  • 10-year yield, WTI and the dollar all up over 3 months tunable?
    • YesCross-asset warning ("three death knells")
  • Fragility composite above 80th percentile tunable? (unprofitable IPOs, HY issuance share, corporate debt growth, buybacks, tight HY spreads, margin debt)
    • YesFragility high: shrinks long size, adds evidence to shorts. Never sets direction
  • Also: 10-year vs nominal GDP growth, financial conditions (NFCI), fiscal supply, cross-region balance sheets

Step 1b · Market internals

Stocks lead the economy; read as evidence feeding the thesis, not as confirmation after it.

  • Leading industries (homebuilders, transports, retail, banks, small caps, metals, semis): has their 6-month relative strength tunable turned, confirmed 2 months?
    • Turned upInternals up (6–12 months ahead of the economy)
    • Turned downInternals down
  • Share of industries above trend fell ≥ 20 pts in 6 months while the index rose?
    • YesLeadership narrowing: yellow light, required before any bear market
  • Industries above trend jump from < 20% to > 80% within 10 weeks?
    • YesBreadth thrust: overrides a negative liquidity read for 6 months (the June 2020 lesson)
  • Curve and credit trends (weight halved while QE distorts bonds); momentum bottoms 12–18 months before fundamentals; cross-asset trend vector

Step 2 · Thesis

Change, not level. 25 transition rows map a regime or internals change to a thesis with premises.

  • Did any regime or internals state change at the as-of date?
    • NoHold existing theses whose premises still hold; no new thesis (T24)
    • YesMatch transition table rows (see Thesis table)
      • MatchThesis: class, direction, region, premises, invalidation events, 18–36 month horizon
      • ConflictingLong and short on the same class and region → cash (T25)
  • Forward policy view from the Treasury curve: 2-year − fed funds beyond ±50 bp tunable = cuts or hikes priced (replayable back to 1976)
  • Post-bubble bear markets last > 6 months; currency trends last ≥ 2 years

Step 3 · Expression

Pick what best expresses the thesis across the whole menu.

  • Rank instruments by 3-year weekly beta to the thesis driver
    • ThenAdd second-order expressions in other classes (|corr| ≥ 0.5): the sterling → gilts → UK stocks pattern
      • ThenRank by payoff asymmetry: upside to target ÷ downside to premise break
        • OutTop 3 expressions, long and short; ties go to the more liquid class (rates/FX first)

Step 4 · Veto

"If I really like a fundamental thesis and the chart stinks, I won't do it."

  • Chart quality: do daily, weekly (40-week tunable) and monthly trend mostly agree with the thesis, and relative strength is not bottom-quintile?
    • NoVeto
    • YesPrice vs news: after a thesis-relevant release, did price move against the news?
      • YesVeto (timing weight 0.2: technicals ~20% as effective today)
      • NoLiquid enough? ADV ≥ $50M and exit cost ≤ 1% of NAV at starter size
        • NoVeto
        • YesEquity short?
          • YesRallied ≥ 15% from the low?
            • NoReduce or go flat instead ("just sidestep it")
            • YesAdmit as starter
          • NoAdmit as starter
  • Crowded positioning never vetoes; at most delays entry 5 days ("the crowd makes money 80% of the time")
  • Article gates computed at weight 0: forensics, ROIC, smart money, contrarian COT

Step 5 · Tier and size

Invest, then investigate: a starter goes on first, and size follows the evidence.

  • How many independent evidence families agree? (liquidity regime, policy error, internals, chart after entry, catalyst within 20 business days, asymmetry)
    • ≥ 3 tunable and asymmetry ≥ 5:1Fat pitch: full size (target 1–2 a year)
    • FewerStarter: one third of the class cap. Failed evidence lowers tier, never removes
  • Size band = tier × class cap × liquidity cap × hot/cold × fragility × asymmetry
    • Class capsEquity net +100% / −50% · single currency 150–200% · bonds 300% in 10-year equivalents
    • LiquidityExit cost ≤ 1–2% of the fund
    • Hot / coldUp ≥ 20% YTD → full; down → one third; never bet big to get even; resets 1 January
    • PortfolioGross leverage ≤ 4:1, scaled pro rata

Step 6 · Monitoring and exit

No stop-losses. Exit when the reason changes, the same day.

  • Every business day: is any recorded premise now false?
    • YesExit, naming the broken premise; if the break triggers another row, the new thesis starts the same day (gold sold on election night 2016)
  • Price moved against the news on two consecutive thesis releases?
    • YesExit
  • Winner whose weekly rate of change has been flat 4 weeks and price is > 1 ATR below its 10-week high?
    • YesExit half ("I'm a technician, I usually wait for tops"); never exit on valuation alone
  • After the 2–3 week investigation window, is evidence still at starter level?
    • NoDrop
  • Price-based stop-loss: never

Step 7 · Cash default

"Not to play when you don't see a fat pitch."

  • Did any thesis survive the veto without conflict?
    • No"No pitch": show nearest misses and the vetoes that blocked them
    • YesPitches by tier; everything else stays in cash. No baseline equity allocation
